Step 1

Step 1: Run the 10-Point Admin Bleeding Test

Go through each item. Be honest. If your business does three or more of these, you're leaving six figures on the table every year.

1. Staff re-enter data from one system into another

CRM to Xero. Timesheets to payroll. Job details into a spreadsheet. If someone is copying and pasting between tools, that's process waste.

2. Someone manually chases invoices or payments every week

Sending reminder emails by hand. Checking who's overdue in Xero. Following up with the same wording every time.

3. Timesheets arrive from the field and get typed up in the office

Paper timesheets or text messages from tradies, manually entered into payroll or job costing software.

4. Job schedules are updated manually across multiple tools

Status changed in the project management tool, then again in a spreadsheet, then again in an email to the client. Three updates for one change.

5. Someone compiles a weekly report by pulling from 3–4 different tools

Every Friday, someone spends hours copying data from your CRM, accounting software, and job tracker into one report.

6. The same follow-up email gets sent over and over with different names

Quote follow-ups, job completion updates, onboarding instructions — if the template is the same but someone types it manually each time, that's automatable.

7. You've hired headcount just to handle volume of repetitive work

If you hired a second admin to do the same thing the first admin does, that's not a staffing problem. That's a systems problem.

8. Compliance or safety documents are tracked in spreadsheets

Licence expiry dates, insurance certificates, registration renewals — manually checked and chased.

9. Client updates are sent manually after job milestones

"Job started" email. "Inspection passed" text. "Invoice sent" notification. All typed by hand.

📋 Template
Step 2: Split Process Work from Relationship Work

This is the most important distinction in automation. Get it wrong and you'll waste money automating the wrong things — or worse, damage client relationships.

Process work follows the same steps every time. It's triggered by an event (job completed, invoice due, email received). The outcome is predictable. A new hire could follow the instructions without thinking.

Relationship work requires judgement, trust, and context. Knowing which subbie to call at 6am when a job goes sideways. Negotiating a price with a long-term supplier. Calming down an unhappy client.

Here's the rule: automate the process work so your people can spend more time on the relationship work.

Key Takeaway: If you can write exact instructions for a new hire to follow without thinking, it's process work. If they'd need to "use their judgement," it's relationship work. Automate the first. Protect the second.

Task Type Why
Sending invoice reminders at 7, 14, 21 days Process Same trigger, same steps, same wording
Negotiating a payment plan with a struggling client Relationship Requires empathy, context, judgement
Copying job details from email into your PM tool Process Data moves from A to B, same format every time
Deciding which subcontractor to assign to a tricky job Relationship Requires knowing the subbies personally
Generating a quote from a standard rate card Process Lookup + calculation + template
Scoping a custom project with a new client Relationship Unique every time, requires expertise

Step 3

Step 3: Run the 5-Question Test on Every Task
1
Does it repeat on the same schedule or trigger?** (Weekly payroll, daily email triage, invoice on job completion)
2
Does it follow the same steps every time?** (Same process, same order, same outcome)
3
Does it involve moving data between two digital tools?** (CRM to Xero, email to spreadsheet, form to job management)
4
Is the outcome predictable?** (No judgement call — if X happens, do Y)
5
Could you write exact instructions for a new hire to follow?** (Step-by-step, no "use your best judgement")
If you answered YES to three or more, that task is automatable.

Run this test on every task you identified in Step 1. Write down each one that scores 3+ and move to Step 4.

🔑

Key Takeaway: Don't try to automate everything. The 5-question test filters out the tasks where automation will actually stick versus the ones where it'll break in 30 days.

Step 5

Step 5: Priority Score Each Opportunity

Not all automation is equal. Some tasks are high-impact and simple to automate (quick wins).

Impact (1–10): How much time, money, or risk does this task create? Consider hours saved, error reduction, speed improvement, and revenue impact (e.g., faster quoting = more jobs won).

Complexity (1–10): How hard is it to automate? Consider how many tools are involved, whether the process is standardised, exception rates, and whether clean digital data exists.

Step 6: Check the 5 Zones

When we audit a business, we check five zones in order. Each one has a typical savings range based on what we've found across 50+ engagements.

What to look for in each zone:

Zone 1 — Email: How many emails does your team handle daily? Are they sorted manually? Do standard replies get typed from scratch each time? One client's CEO was spending 3+ hours per day on email — after automation, 45 minutes.

Zone 2 — Quoting: How long does a quote take from enquiry to send? How many steps? A plumbing company took 8 steps and 2–4 hours per quote. After automation: 30–45 minutes. Their quote turnaround went from 24–48 hours to under 1 hour.

Zone 3 — Job Management: Are jobs created manually from emails or calls? Are schedules updated in multiple places? One client was losing an hour every morning just figuring out who's going where.

Zone 4 — Invoicing: How quickly does an invoice go out after a job is done? How do you chase overdue payments? One business reduced accounts receivable days from 52 to 34 — a $70,000 improvement in cash position.

Zone 5 — Reporting: Who builds the weekly report? How long does it take? If someone spends every Friday afternoon pulling numbers from four different systems, that's 200+ hours per year on a task that should run automatically at 6am.

🔑

Key Takeaway: You don't need to automate all five zones at once. Pick the zone with the highest Priority Score from Step 5 and start there. Most businesses start with Zone 1 (email) or Zone 4 (invoicing) because they're quick wins with immediate ROI.

Watch Out

Common Mistakes

Mistake 1: Trying to automate relationship work+

The tradie whose admin team manages 200+ subcontractor relationships doesn't need less people. He needs his people doing less garbage work. Automate the process work. Protect the human work.

Mistake 2: Starting with the most complex process+

Your full estimating pipeline with 47 edge cases is not where to start. Start with invoice follow-ups or email triage. Prove value in 2 weeks. Build momentum.

Mistake 3: Using ChatGPT as your automation strategy+

ChatGPT is a tool, not a system. Pasting invoices into ChatGPT one by one isn't automation — it's a different flavour of manual. Real automation triggers itself, runs without you, and handles exceptions.

Mistake 4: Building from YouTube tutorials+

Templates don't fit real businesses. That n8n tutorial was built for a generic scenario — not your specific Xero setup, your job management tool, or your team's actual workflow. It'll break in 30 days.

Mistake 5: Not measuring before you build+

If you don't know how many hours you're wasting now, you'll never know if the automation worked. Run this checklist first. Get your baseline. Then automate and measure the difference.
